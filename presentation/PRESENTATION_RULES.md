# How to Build a Winning Competition Presentation

A slide-by-slide blueprint for a ~17-minute talk (15 min slides + demo, with buffer) that makes judges believe you should win first place.

---

## Before you build a single slide: 3 rules

These three rules decide whether your presentation feels *sharp* or *average*. Apply them to every slide you make.

1. **One idea per slide.** If a slide is making two points, split it or cut one. Aim for **9–13 slides total** for the whole talk — not 25. A crowded slide makes the judge read instead of listen to you.
2. **Every slide must answer "so what?"** If a judge looking at a slide can't tell why it matters, the slide is decoration. Cut it or sharpen it.
3. **Map your talk to the judging rubric.** This is the single highest-leverage thing you can do. Get the official scoring criteria, and make your sections literally hit each one. If "technical difficulty" is 30% of the score, make sure a whole section earns those points.

> **Timing note:** Budget for **~17 minutes, not 20.** Going over time gets punished, and you want a buffer in case something runs long or a question comes up. Practice with a timer until you land comfortably under.

---

## The full structure (slide by slide)

Here is the complete spine. Each step lists **how long it takes**, **what goes on the slide**, and **what you say / why it works.**

---

### 1. The problem we want to solve — ~2 minutes, 1 slide

**Goal:** Open by making the judge *care*. This is your "why should I pay attention" moment, and it's now the first thing they see — so it has to land.

**What's on the slide:**
- **One specific person or scenario** who hurts because of this problem. Concrete beats market-speak. ("Sarah, a nurse, spends 2 hours a night on paperwork" beats "Healthcare professionals face administrative burden.")
- A sharp framing line — ideally a question or a striking fact, not a description.

**Why it works:** A specific, real pain is something judges can feel. A vague market statistic is something they forget.

---

### 2. What exists today — ~1.5 minutes, 1 slide

**Goal:** Show the current landscape honestly — the tools, workarounds, or manual processes people use right now — and where each one falls short. This sets up *why a new approach is needed* without you having to brag.

**What's on the slide:**
- The **1–2 main alternatives** people actually reach for today (an existing product, or "they just do it by hand").
- For each, the **gap** — the specific thing it can't do or does badly for the person in slide 1.

**Keep it tight:** Do NOT build a 6-row feature matrix. Name the few alternatives that matter and the one gap each leaves open.

**One caution — be honest.** If you claim *"nothing like this exists"* and a judge knows of a competitor, you lose credibility for the *whole* talk. Stronger and safer: *"Today people use A or B — but neither handles C, which is the hard part."* Naming the real landscape accurately makes you look more credible, not less.

**Why it works:** It frames the open space your project fills. The judge now understands the gap *before* you reveal how you close it.

---

### 3. The insight — ~1.5 minutes, 1 slide

**Goal:** This is the slide that separates *clever* from merely *competent*. Judges remember the insight more than your feature list.

**What's on the slide:**
- The **non-obvious idea** at the core of your solution, in one line.
- The format that works: **"Everyone does X. We realized Y."**

**Why it works:** It shows you didn't just build something — you *saw* something others missed. Coming right after "what exists today," it lands as the answer to the gap you just exposed.

---

### 4. What you built — ~2 minutes, 2 slides

**Goal:** Explain your solution in one plain-language breath. "Product language". **No tech jargon yet.**

**What's on the slide:**
- One sentence describing what it does for the user.
- A simple "what it does" visual — a before/after, or a 3-step flow.

**Why it works:** Judges need a clear mental picture of the *what* before they can appreciate the *how*. Don't skip straight to architecture.

---

### 5. How it works — ~3 minutes, 2 slides  ⭐ *Your depth section*

**Goal:** Prove you can build hard things. **This is the section that wins a *programming* competition.** Engineer-judges want evidence you wrestled something genuinely difficult.

**What's on the slides:**
- A **clean architecture diagram** (one slide) — simple boxes and arrows, not every library you used.
- A **zoom-in on the 1–2 hardest things you solved**. Explain the hard part with pride.

**What to avoid:** Do NOT list every framework, language, and tool. That reads as a résumé, not as skill. Pick the hard problems and go *deep* on those.

**Why it works:** Depth on one hard thing impresses far more than breadth across ten easy things.

---

### 6. Demo — ~4–5 minutes  🎯 *The emotional peak*

**Goal:** Show the magic working.

**How to run it:**
- **Tightly scripted.** Show the *core magic* — not a tour of every button and menu.
- **End on the "wow"** — the single most impressive moment.
- **Default safe approach:** a recorded screen capture that you narrate live. It looks polished and removes the risk of live failure.
- **Have the backup video ready** regardless of what you choose.

**Why it works:** This is where belief turns into excitement. A focused demo that nails one impressive thing beats a rambling tour every time.

---

### 7. Vision + close — ~1.5 minutes, 1 slide

**Goal:** Control the final image in the judge's head. End on your terms, not on whatever the demo left behind.

**What's on the slide:**
- Where this goes next — the bigger picture beyond the hackathon.
- **One clean final line.** Memorable, short, confident.

**Why it works:** The last 15 seconds disproportionately shape the judge's overall impression. A controlled, confident close says "this team knows exactly what they have."

## Quick reference: the whole talk at a glance

| # | Section | Time | Slides | Job |
|---|---------|------|--------|-----|
| 1 | The problem we want to solve | ~2 min | 1 | Open by making them care |
| 2 | What exists today | ~1.5 min | 1 | Show the gap, honestly |
| 3 | The insight | ~1.5 min | 1 | Show you saw what others missed |
| 4 | What you built | ~2 min | 1–2 | Plain-language solution |
| 5 | How it works | ~3 min | 2–3 | Prove technical depth ⭐ |
| 6 | Demo | ~4–5 min | — | The magic, the peak 🎯 |
| 7 | Vision + close | ~1.5 min | 1 | Control the final impression |

**Total: ~15–17 minutes, ~9–13 slides.**

---

## Final checklist before you present

- [ ] Did I get the judging rubric and map my sections to it?
- [ ] Is there exactly one idea on each slide?
- [ ] Does my problem slide open with a *specific* person, not a vague market?
- [ ] Does "what exists today" name the real alternatives and stay honest?
- [ ] Can I state my core insight in one line?
- [ ] Does my "how it works" go *deep* on 1–2 hard things, not list every tool?
- [ ] Is my demo scripted to show the core magic and end on a "wow"?
- [ ] Do I have a recorded backup video of the demo?
- [ ] Is my final line short, confident, and memorable?
- [ ] Have I rehearsed with a timer and landed under 17 minutes?
